Overview

This e-Portfolio is a compilation of my evaluated works in the Security and Risk Management module. It comprises artefacts from both units, my personal achievements, a comparison between Unit 6 and Unit 11, and a 1,000-word reflective comment.

  • Harvard referencing throughout.
  • Clear layout and easy to read.
  • Presentation of learning outcomes that are based on practice evidence.

Artefacts

Modules 1–5 – The Basis of Security and Awareness in a Digital Society

Researched Industry 4.0 and security risk, seeing what IoT and AI mean for operations and how new threat vectors emerge. Increased awareness of cyber-physical resilience and incident analysis.

Unit 6 – Identifying the Risks and Working with Your Team

Generated a Risk Identification Report in regards to “Pampered Pets”. Utilized NIST Cybersecurity Framework and qualitative risk matrix. Participated in peer assessment to enhance communication and coordination.

Sections 7–10 – Models for Vulnerability Assessment

The CVSS limitations were examined and the SSVC and EPSS were compared for contextualised, data-driven vulnerability prioritisation. Applied concepts in data centre security.

Unit 11 – Final Project: ICS Risk Assessment and DR Plan

Developed a quantitative risk model using Monte Carlo simulation and FMEA for evaluation of supply-chain and quality risks. Implemented an end-to-end Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity Plan.

Contributions

  • Risk Modelling Lead (Unit 11): Created Monte Carlo and FMEA model to quantify risk and suggested an active-active DR design.
  • Risk Identification (Unit 6): Prepared QRA matrix and translated controls to the NIST CSF categories by setting baseline controls.
  • Framework Analysis (Units 7–10): Proposed hybrid SSVC + EPSS model for prioritising vulnerability in data centre operations.

Final piece of Work Assessment – Unit 11 vs Unit 6

This section compares the Unit 6 Risk Identification to the Unit 11 Final Project in order to illustrate developed scope, increased risk maturity and lessons learnt.

Aspect Unit 6 Status Unit 11 Outcome Commentary
Scope On-site actions and qualitative risk analysis (NIST QRA). Quantitative modelling in a global digital supply chain (Monte Carlo + FMEA). Extended from a regional to an enterprise model.
Risks Operational dependency and manual workflows. Automation faults and reliance on a supplier (~10% probability of disruptions annually). Risk level transferred from local to systemic cyber-physical problems.
Controls POS upgrades and basic backups. Cloud replication, predictive analysis and MFA. Controls evolved into layered business resilience.
Continuity Manual backup and recovery processes. Formal DR/BCP with ~1-minute RPO and ready for fail-over. Evolution from natural continuity to engineered stability with metrics.
Lessons Learned Needs consistent risk documentation and role clarity. Qualitative and quantitative methods combined with redundancy planning. An integrated approach to risk starting from technology, then people and process.
