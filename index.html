Overview

How to read this portfolio

This site presents my assessed work for the module Security & Risk Management. It aligns with the marking criteria and includes at least one artefact from each unit, clear evidence of my individual contributions, a comparison between the Unit 11 final project and the Unit 6 status, and a reflective piece capped at 1,000 words.

  • Academic integrity: Harvard referencing throughout.
  • Accessibility: clear headings, alt text, and descriptive links.
  • Evidence-first: screenshots and links to repositories are embedded where relevant.

Artefacts

Evidence of learning and development from Units 1–11

Units 1–5 – Foundation of Security and Digital Risk Awareness

During Units 1–5, I developed my initial understanding of cybersecurity concepts and digital risk awareness through activities on Industry 4.0 and Security Risks. I explored how IoT, AI, and automation reshape industrial operations and introduce vulnerabilities. My contributions included initial and summary discussion posts, critically analysing real-world incidents such as the NotPetya cyberattack and cloud-service outages. This stage built my foundational understanding of how emerging technologies connect with security governance and risk management.

Evidence: screenshots of discussion posts and peer responses.

Unit 6 – Risk Identification and Team Collaboration

In this unit, I collaborated on a team project titled “Risk Identification Report for Pampered Pets.” I applied a Qualitative Risk Assessment (QRA) aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, identifying operational, technological, financial, and market risks. I created risk matrices and mitigation plans covering actions such as POS tracking, staff training, and ERP implementation. Additionally, I completed a peer evaluation reflecting teamwork and communication within Group B.

Evidence: uploaded risk report and peer evaluation template (Unit 6 deliverables).

Units 7–10 – Vulnerability Assessment Frameworks (CVSS, SSVC, and EPSS)

Across Units 7–10, I critically examined and compared vulnerability-scoring frameworks. My analysis identified limitations in CVSS and argued for hybrid adoption of SSVC and EPSS to combine contextual and predictive approaches. Peer engagement expanded the discussion on how combining CVSS with SSVC and EPSS provides balanced, real-world prioritisation of vulnerabilities. This phase strengthened my ability to evaluate and adapt risk models for different organisational contexts.

Evidence: discussion summary posts and peer feedback screenshots.

Unit 11 – Final Project: Quantitative Risk Assessment and Disaster Recovery Plan

The final project synthesised learning from all prior units into a quantitative risk assessment and disaster-recovery strategy for Cathy’s Company. Using Monte Carlo simulation and FMEA, I modelled supply-chain and quality-assurance risks, identifying 10% yearly disruption probability and 2–5% per-shipment quality failures. I designed a multilayered mitigation and DR plan including cloud-based replication, cybersecurity hardening, multi-sourcing, and GDPR compliance. The project demonstrated competency in integrating quantitative modelling, enterprise resilience, and regulatory compliance within a single framework.

Evidence: executive summary report and simulation data tables.

Reflective Piece

≤ 1,000 words

The reflective piece must be no more than 1,000 words. Suggested structure using Rolfe (2001):

  1. What: briefly describe the incident/activity/outcome.
  2. So what: analyse your emotions/behaviours, relate to literature and frameworks (e.g., CVSS, SSVC, EPSS), evaluate implications.
  3. Now what: concrete actions and how learning will inform future practice.

If using video, include English subtitles and upload the transcript file alongside submission.
