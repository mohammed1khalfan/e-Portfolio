Overview

How to read this portfolio

This e-Portfolio presents my assessed work for the module Security & Risk Management. It includes artefacts from each unit, my individual contributions, a comparative evaluation between Unit 6 and Unit 11, and a 1,000-word reflective piece.

  • Academic integrity: Harvard referencing throughout.
  • Accessibility: clear headings and descriptive links.

Artefacts

Evidence of learning and development from Units 1–11

Units 1–5 – Foundation of Security and Digital Risk Awareness

During Units 1–5, I explored Industry 4.0 and Security Risks, understanding how IoT, AI, and automation reshape industrial operations. My posts critically discussed real-world incidents such as the NotPetya cyberattack and cloud-service outages, forming a strong foundation for security governance and risk management.

Evidence: screenshots of discussion posts and peer responses.

Unit 6 – Risk Identification and Team Collaboration

Collaborative project: “Risk Identification Report for Pampered Pets.” Applied a Qualitative Risk Assessment (QRA) with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework to identify operational, technological, and financial risks. Produced mitigation matrices and a peer evaluation reflecting teamwork within Group B.

Evidence: risk report and peer evaluation template (Unit 6 deliverables).

Units 7–10 – Vulnerability Assessment Frameworks (CVSS, SSVC, EPSS)

Critically analysed vulnerability-scoring frameworks. Highlighted CVSS limitations and proposed hybrid integration with SSVC and EPSS for contextual and predictive prioritisation. Peer debates deepened my understanding of risk-model evaluation and practical adaptation.

Evidence: discussion summaries and peer feedback screenshots.

Unit 11 – Final Project: Quantitative Risk Assessment and DR Plan

Developed a quantitative risk assessment and disaster-recovery plan for Cathy’s Company. Used Monte Carlo simulation and FMEA to quantify supply-chain and quality risks, designing cloud-based DR, cybersecurity hardening, and GDPR-compliant resilience strategies.

Evidence: executive summary report and simulation tables.

Final Project Evaluation

Unit 11 vs Unit 6

This section compares the Unit 6 Risk Identification with the Unit 11 Final Project, showing scope evolution, risk maturity, and lessons learned.

AspectUnit 6 StatusUnit 11 OutcomeCommentary
Scope Local operations and qualitative risk review (NIST QRA). Global digital supply chain with quantitative modelling (Monte Carlo + FMEA). Expanded from local analysis to enterprise-wide modelling.
Risks Operational dependency, limited backup, manual workflow. Automation defects, supplier dependency, 10 % yearly disruption risk. Risk scale shifted from local to systemic cyber-physical issues.
Controls POS upgrades, staff training, basic backups. Cloud replication, predictive analytics, MFA, compliance controls. Controls matured into layered enterprise resilience.
Continuity Manual backup and recovery. Formal DR/BCP with 1-min RPO and active-active failover. Shifted to engineered continuity with measurable metrics.
Lessons Learned Need consistent risk documentation and role clarity. Integrate qualitative + quantitative analysis, plan redundancy, ensure compliance. Holistic risk strategy combining technology, people, and process.
