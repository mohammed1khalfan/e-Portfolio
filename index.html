Overview

This e-Portfolio is a collection of my assessed projects from the Security and Risk Management module. It consists of work from both units, my achievements, an activity comparing Unit 6 and Unit 11, and a written reflection of 1,000 words.

  • Harvard referencing throughout.
  • Clear format and easy to read.
  • Reporting learning achievements aligned with practice evidence.

Artefacts

Modules 1–5 – Foundation in Security and Awareness for the Digital Age

Researched Industry 4.0 and security risk – what IoT and AI can do to operations and how new threats arise. Raised awareness and analysis of cyber-physical resilience incidents.

Section 6 – Assessing the Risks and Working with Your Team

Prepared a Risk Analysis Report on “Pampered Pets”. Used the NIST Cybersecurity Framework along with a qualitative risk matrix. Engaged in peer evaluation to improve communication and coordination.

Parts 7–10 – Vulnerability Assessment Models

The limitations of CVSS were analyzed, and SSVC and EPSS were evaluated for contextualised, data-driven vulnerability prioritisation. Included discussion on data wrangling in data centre security.

Unit 11 – ICS Risk Assessment and DR Plan

Created a risk model (Monte Carlo simulation and FMEA) to evaluate supply-chain and quality risks quantitatively. Developed an end-to-end Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity Plan.

Contributions

  • Risk Modelling Lead (Team 11): Developed a high-quality model with Monte Carlo and FMEA calculations for risk analysis. Recommended an active-active design as DR.
  • Risk Identification (Unit 6): Developed a QRA matrix and converted it to NIST CSF categories, establishing baseline controls.
  • Integration of SSVC and EPSS (Units 7 and 10): Proposed a hybrid SSVC + EPSS model to prioritize vulnerabilities in data centre operations.

Final Piece of Work Assessment – Unit 11 vs Unit 6

This section compares the Unit 6 Risk Identification to the Unit 11 Final Project to demonstrate that the scope has matured, risk maturity has increased, and key lessons have been learned.

Aspect Unit 6 Status Unit 11 Outcome Commentary
Scope On-location activities and NIST QRA. Quantitative modelling in a worldwide digital supply chain (Monte Carlo + FMEA). Expanded from a local to an enterprise level.
Risks Operational dependency and manual workflows. Failure of automation and dependency on a supplier (~10% risk for shortages per year). Risk transferred from local to systemic cyber-physical problems.
Controls POS upgrades and basic backups. Replication in the cloud, predictive analytics, and MFA. Controls transformed into more complex business resilience.
Continuity Manual backup and recovery processes. Formal DR/BCP with ~1-minute RPO and readiness for fail-over. Shift from natural continuity to engineered resilience with measurable metrics.
Lessons Learned Requires ongoing risk documentation and role clarity. A redundancy design that includes qualitative and quantitative methods. Unified risk approach integrating technology, people, and processes.
